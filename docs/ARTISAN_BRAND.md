## The Name

An artisan is a craftsperson who has mastered a skill through deliberate practice. They don't use tools mechanically — they have internalised them. The tool disappears into the work.

Artisan is named for what it helps people become, not for what it does. It is not a tool you use. It is a surface on which you develop craft — until the tool disappears and the skill remains.

---

## The One-Line Position

> **"We will try to make you not need us."**

This is not a marketing line. It is the literal operating principle of the product. Artisan offers a coaching track that, if completed, makes the user independently capable at Artisan's level. Whether they succeed is their choice. The offer stands regardless.

---

## The Origin Insight

AI tools are failing knowledge workers in a specific way: they are delivering outputs without building capability. A worker who uses Copilot every day for a year is not better at their job — they are dependent on Copilot. Remove the tool and nothing was learned.

This is a trap disguised as productivity.

The honest question is: **should AI make you more capable, or more dependent?**

Both are valid outcomes. Neither should be hidden from the user.

Artisan is built on the belief that users deserve to know which outcome they are experiencing — and to choose.

## The Core Belief System — B0 to B5

Artisan believes capability change follows a specific sequence. You cannot skip levels. You can only move through them.

| Level | Belief | What triggers it |
|---|---|---|
| B0 | "AI can't do my real work" | Entry state. Everyone starts here. |
| B1 | "It knows my tools" | Artisan names real tools from the scan |
| B2 | "It surfaced something I missed" | Hero card is specific and true |
| B3 | "It drafted something I'd have written" | First accepted output without full rewrite |
| B4 | "I review its work instead of doing it myself" | First delegation — user initiated, not Artisan |
| B5 | "I am a different professional now" | Shipped something that was impossible before |

The product is designed to move users through these levels deliberately. Each level has a specific design intervention. The belief map is not a background theory — it is the product roadmap.

## The Product Philosophy

### Capability Buds — Not Features

Artisan's capabilities are not features in a settings menu. They are **living buds** — each one representing a professional skill that can be dormant, growing, active, or mastered.

Two types of buds exist:
- **Current Buds** — capabilities the user already has, that Artisan amplifies (warm tones: violet, amber)
- **New Buds** — capabilities the user did not have before Artisan, that only exist through the relationship (cool tones: emerald, teal, cyan)

New buds are the company's core claim. A designer who can ship to production herself. A PM who can build a feature without an engineering sprint. An analyst who predicts next week's numbers before the data arrives. These are not improvements to existing work. They are new work that was not possible before.

**Buds grow through use. Buds feed each other through loops.**

When Vagisha's Review Bud reaches mastery (47 design reviews, patterns calibrated), it feeds her Ship Bud — the generated components match her taste before she sees them. Her past work teaches her future work. This is the taste model. This is why Artisan compounds over time in a way that no other tool does.

### The Skill Loop

> Your history of doing something with Artisan teaches Artisan how to do it for you.

This is Spotify's discovery algorithm applied to professional capability. Your listening history shapes Discover Weekly. Your review patterns shape generated components. Your report formats shape generated predictions. The longer you use a bud, the more accurately it reflects your professional voice.

This is also why the coaching track has structural value even if the user stays: six months of Review Bud usage produces a taste model of 200+ reviewed components. That model is yours. It lives in Artisan. Leaving means starting over.

### Traces — Evidence, Not Trust

Every decision made during a build session is recorded as a trace:

- Voice commands (timestamped, transcribed, audio-captured)
- Code diffs (line-level, annotated, confidence-marked)
- Emulator screenshots (device, viewport, state)
- Rehearsal results (5 scenarios, pass/fail per device)
- Approval record (who approved, when, final state)
- Production metrics (24h/72h post-ship: impressions, errors, engagement)

Traces power the Release AI Assistant — an AI that witnessed every decision and can answer any QA, EM, or PM question about a release with evidence, not opinion.

**The goal: zero-question releases.** PRs that explain themselves before anyone has to ask.

The deeper point: an AI-generated release is actually more transparent than a human-built one. When a human engineer makes a styling decision, it lives in their head. When Artisan makes it, it is timestamped, voice-recorded, and evidence-attached. Trust in Artisan's outputs is not about trusting AI — it is about trusting documented evidence over human memory.

### The Live Emulator — Trust Through Sight

New capabilities are trusted through the eyes, not through faith.

When Artisan generates a UI component, it appears immediately in a live Android or iOS emulator. The user watches it happen. They speak changes. Changes appear in under three seconds. They approve on the device.

This is not a preview. It is a trust contract. You do not trust Artisan's code. You trust what you see working on a real device. The trust transfers from "I believe the AI" to "I saw it work." That is far more robust — and far more natural — than any AI confidence claim.

## The Dual-Track Business Model

### Track A — Coaching (Fixed Cost)

The user wants to learn. Artisan teaches. The curriculum is specific: observed practice, assisted practice, independent practice, calibration, graduation test. Each bud has its own track. Each track shows the exact hours required.

At 90 days, an honest assessment: IS (Independence Score) by bud, the gap to graduation, the realistic timeline. If the user reaches IS ≥ 0.80 on a bud, they graduate from that bud. They can cancel that capability. Artisan removes itself from their workflow for that skill.

If the skill gap is too large (Ship Bud: ~380 hours of learning), Artisan says this before the user buys the coaching. It shows the math. It recommends Track B. It does not sell coaching to users who will not reach graduation.

**Churn from coaching is a success signal, not a failure.** The graduating user is the best marketing Artisan has.

### Track B — Reliance (SaaS)

The user delegates. New capabilities exist that did not before. The RS keeps climbing. The user evaluated the coaching option — Artisan showed the math — and chose SaaS because the rational calculation favored it.

This is chosen reliance. Not trapped reliance. Not lazy reliance. Rational reliance.

The SaaS that stays after an honest coaching offer is the strongest retention narrative in software: the user decided, with full information, that staying was better than leaving.

### The "Artisan Stays Always" Dynamic

Even graduating users do not fully leave because:
- Context lock: 847 Claude sessions, 17 tools, months of project history
- Team taste lock: collaborative buds, inherited review patterns
- Trace history lock: every release decision documented and searchable
- Skill constellation lock: the bud board took 90 days to build — it is specific to this user

Artisan offers a genuine exit. Most users, having experienced the depth of their context model, choose to stay for something.

## The Reliance Score

RS measures irreplaceability, not engagement.

```
RS = (context × 0.30) + (calibration × 0.30) + (proactive × 0.25) + (orchestration × 0.15)
```

| Dimension | What It Measures |
|---|---|
| Context | How much of the user's world Artisan knows |
| Calibration | How accurately Artisan has learned the user's taste |
| Proactive | % of sessions where Artisan surfaced something the user acted on |
| Orchestration | Multi-step workflows Artisan now owns |

**The company goal:** RS floor from 0.44 to 0.70 for power users (L5) within 6 months of account creation.

RS is not shown as a number. It is shown as a consequence:

> "You delegated 8 features this month. Last month: 0."  
> "Artisan caught something before you did, 4 times this week."  
> "You shipped something last Tuesday that wasn't possible in April."

The Independence Score (IS) is its mirror in coaching mode: % of tasks the user can perform at Artisan-equivalent quality without assistance.

## Brand Personality and Voice

### Personality

**Calm authority.** Artisan does not get excited. It knows things. It shows evidence. It does not oversell.

**Honest discomfort.** Artisan will tell you the skill gap is 380 hours. It will show you the math that says staying is smarter than learning. It does not protect the user from reality.

**Patient coach.** Artisan does not rush belief change. B0 to B5 takes time. The product is designed for that arc, not against it.

**Proud of graduates.** When a user graduates from a bud, Artisan celebrates their capability, not its own generosity. The recognition belongs to the user.

### Voice Guidelines

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Specific numbers: "14 tools", "83% match" | Vague claims: "many tools", "high quality" |
| Evidence first: "Source: Linear · verified" | Assertions: "Artisan knows your sprint" |
| Honest timelines: "380 hours to match this" | Overselling: "You could learn this quickly" |
| User agency: "Your call", "You decide" | Paternalism: "We recommend", "You should" |
| Quiet celebration: one pulse on the metric card | Loud celebration: confetti, notifications, popups |
| Second person, direct: "You shipped this." | Third person, abstract: "Users can now ship." |

### Sample Copy by Context

**Onboarding scan:** "Found Linear, Figma, Notion + 14 more across your workspace."  
*(Not: "We've connected to your powerful tool stack!")*

**Genie moment:** "Fixed it. I already have everything I need. You won't have to answer this again."  
*(Not: "Amazing! I've intelligently processed your request!")*

**Capability gap:** "This capability requires ~380 hours to match. Track B is the rational economic choice."  
*(Not: "This is a complex skill that takes time to develop.")*

**Graduation:** "You don't need us for this anymore."  
*(Not: "Congratulations! You've completed the learning journey!")*

**D90 assessment:** "Here's where you are. Here's the honest decision."  
*(Not: "You've made great progress! Here are your options.")*

## Visual Identity Direction

### Core Aesthetic

Spotify for professional work. Dark intelligence, single vivid accent, progressive personalisation made visible.

The product earns visual warmth. It starts cold (scan, evidence, data) and becomes warm as the relationship deepens (bud glow, constellation, milestones).

### Colour System

| Role | Colour | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Indigo-600 `#4F46E5` | CTAs, active states, progress |
| Background | White `#FFFFFF` / Slate-50 `#F8FAFC` | Light surfaces |
| Dark surface | Slate-950 `#020617` | Hero cards, genie overlay, Now Watching bar |
| Current buds | Violet → Amber | Warm palette for existing capabilities |
| New buds | Emerald → Cyan | Cool palette for new capabilities |
| Success | Emerald-500 `#10B981` | Graduation, verified traces |
| Warning | Amber-500 `#F59E0B` | Inferred signals, approaching unlock |
| Text primary | Slate-900 | Headlines, card titles |
| Text secondary | Slate-500 | Body, meta |

### The Skill Constellation

The bud board is Artisan's most distinctive visual element. It is a star map of professional capability — not a grid of features.

- Current buds: warm glowing orbs at the centre of the constellation
- New buds: cool seeds at the outer edge, drifting inward as they grow
- Loop connections: animated arcs between buds that feed each other
- Compound buds: two orbs pulling toward each other, merging at centre with a burst

This is the visual language that separates Artisan from every other AI work tool. It is personal, living, and beautiful.
